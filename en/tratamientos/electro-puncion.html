Promotes pain relief, rapid regeneration and greater relaxation of the affected muscles

Electrical dry needling is a deep, invasive needling technique in which a needle carrying an electrical current is inserted percutaneously into the patient's myofascial trigger points, to promote pain relief, rapid regeneration and greater relaxation of the affected muscles.

  • Clinical indications:
    • Neck pain and low back pain.
    • Tendinopathies (patellar, rotator cuff, Achilles, …
    • Myofascial syndrome (psoas, piriformis syndrome, quadratus lumborum, ...)
    • Ankle and knee sprain.
    • Functional headache.
  • Benefits:
    • Eliminates trigger points.
    • Eliminates local and radiating pain.
    • Prevents an injury from becoming chronic.
    • Speeds up the recovery process.
    • Restores myofascial activity.
    • Increases tissue relaxation quickly and for a prolonged period.
    • Increases blood flow.

Frequently asked questions

Does percutaneous electrolysis hurt?

More than dry needling. A current is added to the needle, producing a burning, stinging sensation for as long as it is applied, which is a matter of seconds. The intensity is set to what each person tolerates, and it can be stopped at any moment.

How will I feel afterwards?

The area is usually sore for one to three days, more so than with dry needling. That is expected: the technique deliberately provokes a repair response in the tissue, and that response is felt.

Is there anyone who should not have it?

Yes. It is not used if you have a pacemaker or another implanted electronic device, nor over infected or broken skin, and it needs assessment in pregnancy and in people with epilepsy. Tell us your history at the initial assessment.

What kind of injury is it used for?

Mainly for tendinopathies that have been going on for a while and are not resolving: patellar and Achilles tendon, tennis elbow, rotator cuff. It is meant for tissue that has turned chronic, not for a recent injury.

Is it done with ultrasound?

When the structure calls for it, yes. Seeing the needle on screen along its whole path means treating exactly the tissue we meant to treat, and keeping clear of vessels and nerves.
