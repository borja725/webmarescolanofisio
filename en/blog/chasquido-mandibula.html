My jaw clicks when I open my mouth

  1. Home
  2. ›
  3. Blog
  4. ›
  5. My jaw clicks when I open my mouth

By Mar Escolano Morell · 25 September 2026 · 2 min read

It is one of the things that worries people most and is least often a problem. Many live for years with a click on opening without anything ever coming of it, and just as many arrive at the clinic convinced their jaw is broken.

What the noise is

Between the jawbone and the skull sits a small cartilage disc that acts as a cushion. When you open your mouth, that disc glides along with the movement.

If for whatever reason the disc sits slightly forward and the jaw catches up with it partway through, you get the click. That is all it is: not bone rubbing, not something slipping out of place, not a joint breaking. It is a part settling back as it passes.

That is why the noise tends to happen at the same point in the opening every time, and why it sometimes occurs on closing too.

When it means nothing

An isolated click, with no pain, that does not limit how far the mouth opens and is not getting worse, is a very common finding. A substantial share of the population has one, and most never develop any problem from it.

In those cases the treatment is not to treat the noise. Trying to get rid of a click that is not bothering anyone usually means chasing a symptom that is not the problem, and it can draw more attention to the area than is helpful.

Put simply: if it only clicks, there is probably nothing to fix.

When it is worth looking at

What changes things is not the noise but what comes with it. It deserves an assessment when the click comes with pain on chewing or opening, when the mouth opens less far than it used to, when the noise has changed position or loudness over a short time, or when the jaw veers to one side as it opens.

And one sign should not be ignored: <strong>locking</strong>. That moment when the mouth gets stuck and is hard to open or close. If that starts happening, even for seconds, it is the warning that the disc is no longer just making a noise.

A click that appears suddenly after something specific is also worth checking: a long dental appointment, a knock, an extraction.

What helps in the meantime

First, and this is not a minor point: stop checking it. It is very common to spend the day opening and closing the mouth to see whether it still clicks. That provides no information and does keep the area irritated.

Beyond that, what usually helps is easing the joint's workload while it settles: avoiding very hard or very chewy food, not opening the mouth to its limit when there is no need, and loosening the habit of clenching during the day, which many people discover they have once they start paying attention.

If there is pain or restriction as well, then there is something to treat, and treatment usually combines work on the chewing muscles and on the upper neck, which in this area always go together.

Frequently asked questions

Can the clicking be got rid of?

Not always, and not always necessary. What can be improved is the pain, the opening and the control of the movement. In some cases the noise goes as that happens; in others it stays and stops mattering, because it no longer bothers anyone.

Is it arthritis?

Almost never. Arthritis of this joint exists, but it sounds different: a continuous grating, like grit, not a clean click. And it appears in a different context and at different ages. A clean click in a young person rarely has anything to do with it.

Do I need an X-ray?

For an isolated click, no. X-rays show bone, and what moves here is a cartilage disc that does not appear on one. When imaging is needed, a different test is the right one, and that is decided by the appropriate professional.

Is it related to stress?

It can play a part, mainly through muscle tension and clenching, but the click itself is a mechanical matter of the disc. Calling it only stress oversimplifies something that usually has several causes at once.
